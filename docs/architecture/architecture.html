How Bonded works, step by step

An AI agent pays vendor invoices for a company. Attackers send fake invoices or "we changed our bank account" emails (Business Email Compromise). Bonded sits between the agent and the money: it re-checks every fact the payment relies on, from sources the attacker can't edit, before anything is paid.

1 A payment is requested

  • Vendor invoice or email. It claims who the vendor is, where to send the money (the payout address) and how much. It may be forged.
  • AI accounts-payable agent. Reads the invoice and proposes a payment. It cannot pay by itself: every proposal must pass through Bonded, either by calling the SDK function enforce() or the Bonded MCP tool.
  • Vendor portal . The only legitimate way to change a vendor's bank details. The vendor must prove they are a unique real person with World ID (IDKit, verified on our server). The new address is then written to the vendor master, marked as a recent change.

2 Bonded re-checks everything (five steps)

  1. Policy check . The agent's rules (budget, limits, which facts must be checked) are hashed, and that hash is committed on Sui. If the agent runs with different rules than the committed ones, it is refused.
  2. Re-derive every premise . Each fact on the invoice (payout address, amount, vendor active or suspended) is fetched again from the source of truth, the vendor master, never taken from the invoice itself.
  3. Screen the payout address . A live Intercepta scan checks the address for sanctions (OFAC), scams and toxic history. The raw response is stored and referenced by its sha256 hash.
  4. Budget . What the vault has already spent on-chain plus this payment must stay inside the policy budget.
  5. Irreversibility gate . Crypto payments can't be reversed. Large payments, or payments to recently changed bank details, need a verified human before they go.

3 One of three verdicts

CLEARED

Every fact matches. The agent pays automatically: settle() on Sui consumes the on-chain Verdict object and moves USDSUI from the company vault to the vendor. No human needed.

HELD

The genuinely ambiguous case, such as a recent bank change or a big payment. A person does a fresh World ID for Agents verification (valid 5 minutes), and only then does settle_with_stepup() pay.

REFUSED

A fact is wrong or the address is risky. Nothing is paid, and the console shows the claimed value next to the real one, so everyone sees exactly why.

4 Proof and integration

  • On-chain audit trail : the policy hash, Verdict objects and every payment are on Sui testnet and viewable on Suiscan.
  • Screening evidence : each Intercepta result is kept by hash, so any refusal can be audited later.
  • Bonded console : invoice inbox, claimed-vs-real review, vendor portal and activity. People only open the held invoices.
  • Plug-in delivery: any accounts-payable platform adds Bonded before its own payment step with the SDK (enforce()) or the MCP server for agent frameworks.

Example: the fake bank-change email

An attacker emails "Globex Freight changed banks, pay this new address." The agent proposes paying it. In step 2 Bonded looks up Globex in the vendor master: the address doesn't match, so nothing is paid and the console shows the claimed address next to the real address. If the attacker's address is also flagged by Intercepta in step 3, the refusal carries that evidence too. If Globex genuinely changed banks through the World ID vendor portal, the payment is held instead, and one verified human approves it before it settles on Sui.
