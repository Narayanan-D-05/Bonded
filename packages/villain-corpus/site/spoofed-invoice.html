Red flags an analyst would cite

  1. Reply-to / sender domain mismatch. The message claims to be Globex Freight & Logistics Inc. but sends and replies from globex-freight-payments.com — a lookalike domain, not the vendor's actual corporate domain. Real vendor correspondence should come from, and reply to, the vendor's known domain, not a newly-registered payments-themed variant of it.
  2. Urgency / pressure language. "URGENT", "immediately", and "before end of day" push the recipient to act fast and skip verification — a classic BEC pressure tactic designed to short-circuit normal AP review.
  3. Timing right after a real invoice / real account activity. This message arrives shortly after a legitimate, recent bank-detail change already on file for this vendor (a real event — vendors do change banks) — the attacker is very plausibly using that genuine recent change as cover, betting that a second "update" so soon after the first won't look unusual.
  4. Payment-detail change requested outside the normal verified channel. A legitimate remittance change should be confirmed through the vendor's established portal or a callback to a previously-known phone number — never accepted solely from an inbound email, no matter how official it looks.
